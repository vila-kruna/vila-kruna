# The Complete Local SEO Tutorial

*A single, end-to-end system for ranking a local business in the Google map pack — distilled from Caleb's full A-to-Z guide, his automation/agent walkthrough, and his critique of the "weekend AI build." Every step has the explanation, the reason it works, the actions to execute, and an example.*

Proof points referenced throughout: a Plano TX plumber went from average map position **17 → 2.35 in two weeks** (~$50k new revenue the following month); a Malden MA plumber went from **6.18 avg / 15% top-3 → 3.22 avg / 74% green coverage**; a Chicago client now ranks green across ~5M people.

---

## Part 1 — How local SEO actually works (read this first)

### You are ranking a Google Business Profile, not a website
When someone searches "plumber near me" or "plumber Houston," Google shows a **map with three business listings** at the top. Those are **Google Business Profiles (GBP)** — not websites. **60–70% of all clicks go to those top three.** Rank fourth and you're effectively on page two: invisible.

This is what makes local SEO a different game from regular SEO. In regular SEO you rank your website's URLs. In local SEO **every page on your website exists for one reason: to push your GBP up the map.** Internalize that and everything else follows. Miss it and you'll waste months building the wrong thing.

### The three glasses: proximity, relevance, authority
Google decides the top three using three factors. Picture each business as a glass that has to overflow to rank:

- **Proximity** — how close the searcher is to the business. *You cannot control this.* It's effectively a penalty you overcome with the other two.
- **Relevance** — how thoroughly your site and GBP prove you do the service. Built with **content.** You control this.
- **Authority** — how much the wider web trusts you. Built with **links.** You control this.

A business that's physically far away can still outrank a closer one by filling the relevance and authority glasses until they overflow. **The entire job is maximizing the two factors you control.**

Why this is so winnable: most local competitors do the bare minimum — one GBP category (Google allows 10), zero services listed (they should have 20–30), a profile filled out once three years ago. Their glasses are half-full because they're ignoring the two factors they could control. "They're not playing the game — they don't even know there's a game to play." That's why you can outrank decades-old businesses in weeks.

### Google matches entities, not keywords (since ~2018)
This is the technical engine under "relevance." Your GBP is an entity. Your website is an entity. Every service, every category, your geography — all entities. **Google constantly checks whether they all match each other. The closer they match, the higher you rank. Every gap reduces trust.** So the work is less "stuff keywords onto pages" and more "eliminate the gaps between what your GBP, website, services, and location all claim about the business."

### The one rule that saves you months
**You are not ranking blog posts.** Any agency pitching you weekly blog posts — run, don't walk. You're not chasing readers in other cities. Every page serves the GBP. (More on why agencies push blog posts in Part 12.)

## Part 2 — Step 1: Diagnose with a rank map

**What it is.** Before touching anything, run a **local rank map** — it shows your GBP's ranking position at **169 points across the city** (Caleb uses a tool called LeadSnap; any geo-grid tool works):
- **Green** = top 3 → visible, getting calls.
- **Yellow** = ~positions 4–10 → invisible, but close.
- **Red** = not ranking → Google doesn't believe you do that service in that location.

**Why you never skip it.** Without it you're guessing — which is exactly what weak agencies do when they "start creating content randomly." A typical struggling business is mostly red/yellow with a little green right at its address. That pattern means **the business only wins on proximity** — Google doesn't trust it anywhere else, so it needs relevance and authority.

**The metric that drives the entire strategy — Top 3%.** Take the total map area and calculate what percentage is green (positions 1–3). Only green counts; position 4 gets no clicks. This number later decides whether you build topical or geographic content (Part 8). Get that decision wrong and you waste months.

**Execute:**
1. Set up a geo-grid rank tool on the GBP.
2. Run a map for your main target keyword (e.g. "plumber Houston").
3. Record the Top 3% as your baseline.

## Part 3 — Step 2: Fix the GBP (the 30-minute audit)

This audit takes about half an hour and immediately puts you ahead of ~90% of local competitors. Caleb's claim from 500+ audits: roughly 80% of local businesses could become six-figure just by getting these basics right. There are three recurring mistakes.

### Mistake 1 — Categories
Most businesses pick one primary category and stop. **Google allows up to 10, and every category is another search term you can appear for.** Target **2–4 minimum** (more if genuinely relevant). A plumber with only "plumber" misses "drainage service," "gas installation service," etc.

*How to pick them:* Use **GMB Everywhere** (a free Chrome plugin). Search your keyword on maps.google.com, open a competitor, hit "find more." It shows every category competitors use and how many GBPs use each related one — so you mine strong secondary categories in minutes. Pair it with an AI prompt for strategy. **Trap:** AI invents categories that aren't in Google's fixed dropdown — paste the real category list and tell it to choose only from that.

### Mistake 2 — Services
Most list 1–5. **List at least 20** (30–40+ in competitive markets). Each service is another signal about what you do. Unlike categories, **services is a free text box** — be creative. Critically, **services must be organized under the category they semantically match** (water heater replacement, pipe repair, leak detection under "plumber"). Matching = a relevance signal; mismatching confuses the algorithm. Remember, "Google is just math."

### Mistake 3 — Empty boxes
Google reads **completion signals.** A fully filled profile = active, legitimate business. An empty one signals dead, spam, or an owner who doesn't care. Fill everything:
- **Description** — use all **750 characters** Google allows.
- **Photos** — **20+** (exterior, interior, team, work-in-progress). Phone photos are auto-geotagged.
- **Posts** — weekly (automate in Step 4).
- **Attributes** — check every box that applies; if one doesn't, set it to "no" (veteran-owned, woman-owned, accepts credit cards, free estimates, ADA compliant, etc.).

## Part 4 — Step 3: Make the website match the GBP (consistency signals)

**Why.** Google checks whether your website and GBP are the **same business.** Fail this and you won't rank in a competitive market — Google assumes one is fake or that you don't know your own business. The page that matters most is your **GBP landing page** (the URL in your GBP's website field — usually the homepage).

**The 8 elements that page needs:**
1. **Title tag** = primary category + city → "Houston Plumber — Same-Day Service — Brand." (Over 60% of businesses just use "Home" or their bare name. They're handing you the ranking.)
2. **H1** = primary category + city.
3. **Google Maps embed** of the GBP location.
4. **Secondary categories + core service** mentioned, ideally in subheadings.
5. **Review widget** displaying Google reviews.
6. **Address** matching the GBP **character-for-character.**
7. **Phone** matching **character-for-character.**
8. **LocalBusiness schema** on the homepage.

**The bonus reason this matters — AI search.** Google invented the transformer in 2017 (the "T" in ChatGPT). Google and ChatGPT check the same thing: **consistency** — does the address match, are services clearly listed, is the info coherent. So fixing this for Google simultaneously makes you recommendable by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude. And an AI recommendation lands like a friend's referral — those customers are "sold before they even call." (Also worth knowing: studies show AI-sourced traffic converts *better* than raw Google traffic — higher intent.)

## Part 5 — Step 4: Automate GBP management (activity signals)

**Why.** Google rewards active profiles over dormant ones. But nobody should spend two hours a week on this by hand. Connect a management tool (Caleb uses LeadSnap) and automate:
1. **Weekly GBP posts** — one prompt generates 52 (a year's worth); schedule them all at once.
2. **Weekly geotagged photo uploads** — Google strips metadata, yet geotagged photos still correlate with more rank movement (phones geotag by default, so Google expects it; tools geotag on upload anyway).
3. **AI review responses** — ~25 seconds to set up, then fully automatic.
4. **Automated citations** — Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, and the major directories.

Set up once (~1 hour); the GBP then stays active all year untouched.

## Part 6 — Step 5: Build the website (the Core 30)

**The structure.** With 3–4 categories and 20–30 services, you'll naturally end up with **~30 pages**: 1 homepage + 3–4 category pages + 25–30 service pages. These 30 pages are the **topical-relevance foundation** Google needs before it ranks you competitively. The hierarchy **mirrors the GBP exactly.**

**The link mechanic that makes it work — editorial links.** Pages are connected by links inside body paragraphs, not the menu. This distinction is critical:
- **Navigation and footer links pass almost no authority** — Google knows they're just site structure.
- **Editorial links (inside content paragraphs) pass real authority** — Google reads them as one page endorsing another.

**The three page types:**
- **Homepage** = the GBP landing page (all 8 consistency elements from Step 3). Then: each secondary category gets an **H2**, 50–100 words explaining it (what it is, why customers need it, what services fall under it, how good you are), and an **editorial link to that category's page.** → 3–4 editorial links out.
- **Category page** — target keyword = **category + city** ("Drainage Service Houston") in the title tag and H1. List the relevant services, each as an H2 with 50–100 words and an **editorial link to that service page.** → 5–10 editorial links out.
- **Service page** — target keyword = **service + city** ("Water Heater Replacement Houston"). Detailed, specific content: why customers need it, what's included, how long it takes, what to expect. This is *not* generic advice ("5 ways to tell if your water heater is failing") — it's the specific service in the specific city.

So: homepage → category pages → service pages, mirroring the GBP. Google sees a comprehensive, well-organized site matching exactly what the GBP already claims. That *is* topical relevance — 30 pages proving you do what you say.

**On AI-written content.** Google doesn't care whether content is AI or human — it cares whether it's **helpful.** Most AI content fails because it's generic walls of text. So build with AI, then have a **human editor** add: real business voice and personality, images (client photos best, AI images acceptable), callouts, tables, proper headers, bullets, short paragraphs, and the internal links. Google evaluates pages on **mobile Chrome** — if a page looks like AI slop with no formatting or images, Google treats it as slop, and increasingly won't even index it. (Part 12 shows the writing pipeline that prevents this at scale.)

## Part 7 — Step 6: Build authority (two kinds of links)

Since the November 2023 flood of AI content, Google leans even harder on links to separate quality from slop — the original 1998 PageRank insight still holds: quality sites don't link to spam. **AI content needs external links to be trusted.** You need two types.

**Type 1 — "This is not AI slop" links.** Medium-quality external links that validate a page is worth reading. They don't have to be local or high-authority — just reasonable sites. **One per page, minimum.** Core 30 = 30 such links; each supporting and geographic page later needs one too. (Caleb sells these ~$35/link; he frames the cost against the six-figure value of a top-three ranking.)

**Type 2 — Local authority links.** Links from local organizations that signal you're a real, trusted business in the community:
- **Chamber of commerce = the single best one.** ~$200–300/yr; they list you in their directory and link back. Google recognizes chambers as local authorities. **Secret: join multiple** — any chamber within ~70 miles will take you (they don't care if you're 30 miles outside the city as long as you pay). Caleb has had clients in 10.
- **Sponsorships and donations** — youth sports leagues, local charities, community events. Costs money, but you get real local links *and* genuine community ties.

## Part 8 — Step 7: The decision framework (topical vs geographic)

This is the hinge of the whole system, and the step most agencies get backwards. **30 days after launching the Core 30**, start running rank maps again and use Top 3% to decide what to build next.

1. **Find your goal.** Run rank maps for the **top 3–4 competitors** and read their Top 3%. In competitive markets the leaders top out around 28–40% (Houston's #1 plumber sits at 28.5%). In soft markets they hit 90–99% (Gary IN's #1 = 94%). **Your goal is to match the leaders in *your* market.**
2. **Set your topical-relevance threshold = 25–50% of that goal.** Leaders at 80% → threshold ~30–40%. Leaders at 40% → threshold ~15–20%.
3. **Decide:**
   - **Below threshold** → Google doesn't yet believe you're an expert → build **topical relevance** (Step 8).
   - **At or above threshold** → Google trusts your expertise → switch to **geographical relevance** (Step 9).

Why this matters: agencies that build location pages *before* proving topical authority get nothing (Google doesn't trust them yet), and agencies that grind FAQs *forever* after authority is established are wasting effort that should go to expanding the radius. The rank map removes the guessing.

## Part 9 — Step 8: Topical relevance (when you're below threshold)

When Google doesn't believe you're an expert, more service pages won't help — you need **supporting content that answers specific questions**, the way a real expert fields detailed questions. These are **not** generic blog posts ("history of plumbing") and **not** city-name-swap pages — those don't work.

**Two question sources:**
- **People Also Ask.** Search your target keyword *without* the city, expand the PAA box repeatedly until you have 20–30 questions. **Do not copy them verbatim** — SEOs abused this so heavily that Google now flags it. **Reword** each (same meaning, different phrasing — transformer tech understands): "How much does it cost to replace a water heater?" → "What's the typical price for water heater replacement?"
- **Reddit.** A prompt crawls Reddit for real local questions about your service in your city. These are gold — hyper-specific and genuinely from real people, not generic PAA.

**How to deploy each FAQ (the pattern):**
1. Add the question to the **most relevant existing page** (general plumbing → homepage or main category page; water-heater-specific → water heater page).
2. Answer it **briefly** — a couple dozen words.
3. Inside that answer, add an **editorial link** to a **new supporting page** that answers the question **in depth** (hundreds of words). Example: *"Water heater replacement typically runs $2,000–$4,000 in Houston depending on type and install complexity. For a full breakdown of pricing factors, see our complete guide to water heater replacement costs."*
4. Every supporting page needs **one "not AI slop" link.**

This adds value to your main pages without bloating them, and builds internal links from the Core 30 into deeper content — proving depth. Keep producing supporting content and running monthly rank maps until you hit your Top 3% threshold.

## Part 10 — Step 9: Geographic relevance (when you're at/above threshold)

Now you stop proving expertise and start **expanding how far from your address you can rank.**

**Target positions 4–6, not the dead zones.** It's far easier to move a 4→3 than a 19→3, and only top three matters — moving 19→4 gets you nothing.

**Method:**
1. On the rank map, zoom into the 4/5/6 cells using a **Google Maps interface** — so you only use areas Google itself recognizes. "If it's on Google Maps, it's relevant; not areas I made up." (At scale these landmarks come straight from the Google Places API — not guesswork.)
2. Pick **landmarks** in those cells: neighborhoods, parks, major intersections, shopping centers, schools, lakes. Only where customers **actually live** — good residential demographics, not the middle of a lake.
3. Target keyword = **service + landmark + city** ("Plumber Harvest Bend the Meadows Houston") → title tag + H1.
4. Write content **genuinely about that area** — not "we serve Memorial Park" fifty times. Real local detail: older homes with cast-iron pipes vs new builds with PEX, nearby businesses, the route technicians drive to get there. (AI does this well — feed it the rank-map CSV plus demographic context.)
5. Every geographic page needs **one "not AI slop" link.**
6. Internally link them: create a **locations page** linking to all geographic pages, and have each geographic page link back to its supporting service page (water heater replacement Memorial Heights → water heater replacement page).

Run the rank map every couple of weeks. As authority grows, new areas appear in positions 4–6 — target those next. You'll watch the green zone spread outward from the address.

## Part 11 — Step 10: The continuous loop and scaling

Where most agencies drop the ball: they think the work is done, stop monitoring, and three months later a still-active competitor pushes them out of the top three.

**Every month:**
- Run the rank map; check Top 3%.
- Below threshold → more topical content. At threshold → more geographic content. Dominating → maintain and defend.
- Keep the GBP active (automated posts/photos), respond to every review, add new services as the business evolves, watch for competitor movement.

**Scaling to new locations:** new location → new GBP → a fresh Core 30 + supporting + geographic content for that area. The fundamentals never change — the three glasses, Core 30, supporting content, geographic content, quality links + local trust links.

## Part 12 — Doing it in a single session: how to run this at scale

Everything above is the manual method — what an agency does by hand over weeks. The same system can be compressed into roughly a **90-minute agent run at under $1/page in API costs.** This is the layer that turns the tutorial into a production line. (Reference economics: agencies traditionally spend 60+ hours and thousands in labor; even AI-assisted by hand it's 30–45 min/page × ~30 pages.)

**The pipeline, end to end:**

1. **Entity research** (maps to Steps 2–3). Pull the GBP's categories, run a **category audit** — literally count how many businesses around the city use each category ("plumber" 68×, "electrician" 17×, "heating contractor" 14×…). Every category competitors use that the client doesn't is a search you're handing them. Select the ones that legitimately fit.

2. **Gap analysis** (maps to Step 5). Parse the GBP's categories/services, **crawl the existing site**, map each page to its category/service, then list everything with no page. That list becomes the build queue. (Most agencies skip this — it's why clients stall for months.)

3. **Relevance research** (maps to Steps 8–9). For topical: scrape PAA, competitor headlines, Reddit and local forums; score each query for relevance; keep the on-intent ones. For geographic: upload the rank map, pull landmarks near the 4–6 cells from the **Google Places API**, with reasoning per cell (distance/direction from the GBP, competitor density, "rank 6 — one optimized page could push top three").

4. **The 8-pass writing pipeline** — the part that makes AI content actually rank instead of getting filtered. One-shot AI writing fails because it's one prompt, one model, one pass → one flat tone the whole way. Humans write section by section, revise, and vary. A short **guardrails prompt** rides on top of every pass, and the engine differs for each content type (service / category / location / supporting). The passes:
   1. **Research synthesis** → compress raw research into a structured content brief (questions to answer, local details to weave in, competitor angles to beat).
   2. **Strategic outline** → full page architecture: every H2, each section's job, top-to-bottom flow (skipping this is why most AI content "wanders").
   3. **Section draft** → each H2 written with its *own* API call, so tone/angle vary slightly section to section, like a human drafting over a day.
   4. **Burstiness** → vary sentence and paragraph length; mix short punchy lines with longer ones.
   5. **Perplexity injection** → replace predictable AI phrasing and favorite words ("robust," "leverage," "streamline," "significant improvements") with how a human actually talks; removes em-dashes.
   6. **Human bookends** → rewrite the first two and last two sentences in extra-conversational, opinionated language — these are what Google and readers weight most heavily (people read the top, then jump to the bottom).
   7. **Conversion pass** → naturally inject CTAs and phone numbers so the page makes the phone ring; customized per content type.
   8. **Final QC** → re-check the whole piece against the brief, outline, guidelines, word count, and any leftover AI patterns; flag and rewrite anything off.

5. **Parallel builds** — while the 8 passes run, the agent simultaneously generates the FAQ section, meta title/description/H1, schema markup, images (with specific prompts), and inserts relevant external authority links.

6. **Auto-deploy** — render a video script, generate the video, create the YouTube title/tag/description, upload to YouTube, and publish to WordPress with the video embedded. Videos measurably improve indexing, ranking, and authority.

7. **Quality control at scale** — every article gets an **AI-detection score.** Rule of thumb: under ~10% is fine; ~65% needs another pass. With this in place, the human's job shifts from copy-paste prompting to **reviewing and double-checking** — the actual bottleneck once production is automated.

You don't need to build all of this to start. Even a lighter version (entity audit + Core 30 + a few links, generated with an AI writing pass and a human editor) gets a soft-market business a long way. The full machine is for competitive metros.

## Part 13 — Reality checks and what NOT to do

**The competitiveness caveat (the most important one).** This system works *fastest* in soft niches against old, slow competitors — diesel repair in a mid-size market, not "plumber" or "HVAC" or "personal injury attorney" in a major metro. A vibe-coded site can absolutely rank in a low-competition niche; it hits a ceiling the moment you enter a competitive market, where you need the full discipline above (entity alignment, GBP-mirrored structure, rank-grid-driven geo relevance, real authority links). The arbitrage is real but it narrows as more local businesses adopt AI — which is exactly why now is a good time to move.

**Don't do these:**
- **Weekly blog posts.** They don't serve the GBP. When Caleb posts this, agencies reply asking "if not blog posts, what do we charge for?" — which tells you their priority is billing, not your rankings.
- **City-name-swap pages** ("plumber Houston," "plumber Katy," "plumber Sugar Land" with find-replace). Google's **August 2025 update flags these as spam**; sites relying on them lost traffic overnight. It's geo-trivia, not geo-relevance.
- **Random internal linking** by topical "feel." Links must follow the GBP hierarchy exactly — don't link a service under one category to a service under a different category. Google builds its picture of your site from internal links, not URL structure.
- **Letting AI pick Google categories** — it invents ones that aren't in the dropdown. Feed it the real list.
- **Treating one screenshot as proof of ranking.** One data point at one location says nothing about how you rank three or five miles away. The rank grid is the real measure.

## Appendix — Quick-reference checklist

**Foundation**
- [ ] Run a baseline rank map; record Top 3%.
- [ ] GBP: 2–4+ relevant categories (GMB Everywhere + AI from the real list).
- [ ] GBP: 20+ services, organized under matching categories.
- [ ] GBP: 750-char description, 20+ geotagged photos, all attributes, products.
- [ ] Landing page: all 8 consistency signals (title, H1, Maps embed, secondary categories/core service, review widget, exact address, exact phone, LocalBusiness schema).
- [ ] Automate posts (52/yr), weekly geotagged photos, AI review responses, citations.

**Build**
- [ ] Core 30: homepage + 3–4 category pages + 25–30 service pages, mirroring the GBP.
- [ ] Editorial links: homepage → categories → services (in-paragraph, not nav/footer).
- [ ] Human-editor pass on every page (voice, images, formatting, tables, internal links).
- [ ] One "not AI slop" link per page (~30 to start).
- [ ] Local authority links: chamber(s) of commerce (join multiple within ~70 mi), sponsorships, charities.

**Grow (monthly loop)**
- [ ] Re-run rank maps; read competitors' Top 3% → set goal → set threshold (25–50% of goal).
- [ ] Below threshold → topical: reworded PAA + Reddit FAQs, brief answer + editorial link → in-depth supporting page (+1 external link each).
- [ ] At/above threshold → geographic: target positions 4–6, Google-Maps landmarks, genuinely-local content, locations page + back-links (+1 external link each).
- [ ] Keep GBP active, respond to all reviews, add services, watch competitors.

**Prompts referenced (build your own library):** strategic category selection (from the real list), service generation by category, content generation for each page type, PAA/Reddit FAQ mining with "which deserve a dedicated page" flags, geographic content prioritization from rank-map CSV + demographics.
